# AI Editorial and SEO Instructions for GearUp.my

Use these instructions whenever generating, reviewing, or updating photography and camera content.

## Core Goal

Create genuinely useful photography content that earns search traffic because it helps readers choose, use, or compare camera gear. AI should assist research, structure, editing, and fact-checking. It must not replace first-hand evidence or invent experience.

## Anti-Thin-Content Rules

- Do not mass-publish near-identical articles from a template.
- Do not create pages that only repeat manufacturer specifications.
- Do not add paragraphs just to reach a word count.
- Merge overlapping articles instead of creating several weak pages for similar keywords.
- Use `noindex` for empty categories, duplicate filters, internal search pages, unfinished drafts, and tag pages with little unique value.
- Every indexable article must answer a distinct reader intent and include meaningful information unavailable from a generic product listing.

## Required Evidence for Reviews

Clearly label the source of every conclusion:

- First-hand testing: only claim this when the editor actually tested the camera or lens.
- Official specifications: verify against the manufacturer documentation.
- Lab or measurement results: name the test source and date.
- Community or owner feedback: describe it as reported feedback, not personal experience.
- Editorial inference: explain how the conclusion was reached.

Never claim to have owned, held, tested, photographed with, or personally compared gear unless that happened.

## Useful Original Value

Where possible, include at least three of these:

- Original sample images or clearly licensed images.
- ISO, autofocus, stabilization, battery, rolling-shutter, or overheating observations.
- Lens compatibility and upgrade paths.
- Real Malaysian pricing with a checked date and named retailers.
- Used-market considerations.
- A comparison with realistic alternatives.
- Best use cases and who should avoid the product.
- A repeatable test method.
- Firmware version and equipment used for testing.
- Practical workflow notes for travel, events, wildlife, vlogging, or studio work.

## Article Structure

Use a structure appropriate to the search intent, not one rigid template:

1. Clear answer or verdict near the top.
2. Who the product is for.
3. Key strengths and limitations.
4. Evidence and testing details.
5. Comparisons or alternatives.
6. Price and value context.
7. Final recommendation.
8. Related GearUp.my links that genuinely help the reader.

Use descriptive headings, concise paragraphs, tables only when they improve comparison, and accurate pros and cons.

## SEO Rules

- One primary intent per page.
- Write a natural title containing the product and the reader's likely purpose.
- Keep title and meta description accurate, specific, and non-clickbait.
- Use related terms naturally: camera model, sensor, autofocus, stabilization, video, lenses, low light, price, and use case.
- Do not repeat the same keyword unnaturally.
- Use stable, descriptive slugs.
- Add canonical URLs, Open Graph metadata, Article schema, and breadcrumb schema where supported.
- Link to relevant category, comparison, buying-guide, and product pages.
- Do not create long-tail pages unless there is enough unique inventory and useful content to support them.
- Update old articles when prices, firmware, availability, or competing models change.
- Show a `last checked` or `last updated` date when facts can change.

## Research and Sourcing

Before writing factual claims:

1. Check the manufacturer's official product page or manual.
2. Check current Malaysian prices from named retailers or marketplaces.
3. Check firmware and availability dates.
4. Cross-check important claims with at least one independent source.
5. Record source URLs and access dates in the draft or editorial notes.

Do not scrape or republish copyrighted reviews, photos, test charts, or descriptions. Summarize in original language and use images only when licensed or owned.

## AI Workflow

Use a controlled batch process:

1. Generate 5 to 10 outlines, not hundreds of finished pages.
2. Review titles and search intent.
3. Gather evidence and source links.
4. Draft articles with the evidence attached.
5. Run a fact-check pass for specifications, prices, dates, and comparisons.
6. Add original editorial notes, images, or test results.
7. Check for duplicate wording against existing GearUp.my articles.
8. Publish a small batch.
9. Review Search Console impressions, clicks, CTR, indexing, and engagement.
10. Expand only after the first batch proves useful.

For bulk generation, output drafts to a review queue. Do not publish directly to production.

## Quality Gate Before Publishing

An article is ready only when all answers are yes:

- Does it solve a specific reader problem?
- Is the verdict supported by evidence?
- Are specifications and prices verified?
- Are personal claims honest and attributed correctly?
- Does it contain unique information or analysis?
- Does it link to useful related GearUp.my pages?
- Is the title accurate rather than exaggerated?
- Is the page meaningfully different from existing articles?
- Are images owned, licensed, or properly credited?
- Would a photographer save or share this article?

If the answer to several questions is no, improve the article, merge it, or keep it out of the index.

## Recommended AI Prompt

> Act as a careful photography editor for GearUp.my. Write an evidence-based draft for the specified search intent. Do not invent first-hand testing, ownership, sample images, prices, specifications, or quotes. Separate verified facts from editorial inference. Use the supplied source links and notes. Identify missing evidence instead of guessing. Produce a concise title, meta description, outline, article draft, pros and cons, alternatives, internal-link suggestions, and a fact-check list. The draft must provide unique practical value and must not resemble a mass-generated doorway page.
