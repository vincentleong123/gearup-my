On 15 September, Canon quietly dropped a compact full-frame for beginners. Nine days later Nikon answered with one of its own. And this month, both actually arrive on shelves in Malaysia — right before the country's biggest online sale of the year.

**Nikon Z5IIC:** announced 24 September 2026, in stores **mid-October** at US$1,399.95 body-only (~RM6,200–6,600) or US$1,699.95 with the 24-50mm kit.

**Canon EOS R8 Mark II:** announced 15 September 2026, and by Canon's usual six-week rhythm, **late October to November** is when bodies land in shops — exactly on top of 11.11.

Add the fact that year-end is graduation and wedding season — the two highest-paying gig months for Malaysian shooters — and the next 30 days form a very unusual buying window. Here is how we'd play it.

## Nikon Z5IIC vs Canon R8 Mark II: Specs, Malaysia Prices and Release Dates

Both are "casual full-frame" cameras: smaller, friendlier and cheaper than flagships, aimed at people stepping up from phones or starter APS-C bodies.

![A reviewer holding a compact black camera up to check it while comparing the Nikon Z5IIC and Canon R8 Mark II](/blog/nikon-z5iic-canon-r8-mark-ii-specs-compare.jpg "Nikon Z5IIC vs Canon R8 Mark II: specs, Malaysia prices and release dates at a glance")

| | Nikon Z5IIC | Canon R8 Mark II |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Announced | 24 Sep 2026 | 15 Sep 2026 |
| Malaysia arrival | mid-October 2026 | late Oct–Nov 2026 |
| US price | $1,399.95 body | ~$1,800 |
| MYR estimate | ~RM6,200–6,600 | ~RM8,000–8,400 |
| Sensor | proven Z5II line, low-light tuned | 24.2MP + IBIS added |
| Kit bundles | 24-50mm, or 26mm/40mm pancake soft bundles | with compact RF zooms |

Two things matter more than the spec sheet. First: the Z5IIC's whole pitch is **simplicity** — scene modes over menus, the same strategy Sony rode with the ZV line. Second: the R8 Mark II adds **IBIS** where the original R8 had none — that single change converts 90% of handheld café, event and street work.

## Buy Now or Wait? 3 Ways to Play the Malaysia Camera Buying Window

### Play 1: Buy a Used Canon EOS R8 Before Prices Drop

When a replacement is announced, the previous body doesn't lose value slowly. In Malaysian classifieds it usually falls **10–20% within weeks**. A used EOS R8 (still excellent for stills) that sat at ~RM4,500 in good shape heads toward **RM3,700–4,000** as seller stock clears shelves. That is RM2,000+ saved vs. the R8 Mark II — enough for the RF 50mm f/1.8 and a Godox V860 III with change left over.

Read our full [Canon EOS R8 review](/gear/canon-eos-r8-review-malaysia) to see if the original still fits your work.

### Play 2: Wait for Nikon Z5IIC Stock and 11.11 Camera Sale Bundles

Brand-new bodies rarely get 11.11 discounts by *value* — instead watch bundles. Nikon's 26mm f/2.8 pancake and 40mm f/2 soft bundles are the tell: if the 11.11 bundle price (body + pancake) lands under **RM7,000**, that is a genuinely good entry into full-frame Z-mount, because those two primes are the street-walk setup half of KL's TikTok was built on.

### Play 3: Skip Both — a Used APS-C and Fast Prime Still Wins

The uncomfortable truth this site keeps repeating: a used A6400 (RM1,400–2,000) plus a Viltrox 56mm f/1.4 (RM550–800) beats both new bodies for actual paid graduation work until you outgrow your clients, not your camera. If your 2026 goal is income, the gear ROI chart doesn't move for either new body.

## 11.11 Camera Sale Malaysia: 4 Tips to Get the Best Camera Price

![A Malaysian shopper compares camera prices on a laptop beside a mirrorless camera at night](/blog/nikon-z5iic-canon-r8-mark-ii-1111-sale.jpg "Track camera prices from October so you can spot a real 11.11 discount from an inflated one")

1. **Track the price, not the flash text.** 11.11 "discounts" on new cameras frequently start from an inflated anchor. Screenshot current shop prices in October.
2. **Used sellers copy the sale too.** Mudah and Carousell listings spike in quantity during the first 2 weeks of November as people fund upgrades — seller-side pressure pushes *their* price down. That's your second window.
3. **Graduation season timing.** Convocations peak late October–December. If you intend to *earn* with the camera, buying by early November means graduation season pays for it twice over this year. Run the numbers yourself in the [ROI calculator](/calculator).
4. **Grey-market Z5IIC bodies** will flood in by December from parallel importers — real savings, but local warranty matters for shutters and IBIS. Know what you're trading.

## Verdict: Should You Wait for the Nikon Z5IIC or Canon R8 Mark II?

![A graduation mortarboard cap beside a mirrorless camera with a fast prime lens on a wooden table](/blog/nikon-z5iic-canon-r8-mark-ii-convocation-gig.jpg "Buying before mid-November means your camera earns during convocation and wedding season")

- **You shoot paid gigs now:** buy used Play 1 or Play 3 today. The Z5IIC's arrival doesn't change your income math; your portfolio does.
- **You're a deliberate beginner stepping to full-frame:** wait for the Z5IIC bundles — simplicity + pancake primes is an honest first full-frame kit.
- **You're a Canon starter:** used R8 now, or hold to December for R8 Mark II's first price cuts. Both are sensible; the only wrong move is buying at the mid-December retail premium.

The next 30 days are noisy. Decide with numbers, not news cycles.
